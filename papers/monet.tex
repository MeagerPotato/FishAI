\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The FishAI project's previous cross-engine paper \cite{frontier} measured its agent at $27.08\%$
against SESTINA v1.0, the frontier bot of the FishLab project \cite{fishlab}, played inside their
C++ engine. This paper reports what a new agent line built to beat that number --- Monet, seventeen
rungs from v0.1 to the acceptance read --- actually bought, under a protocol written before the
first rung ran: every rung's acceptance test written before it and, from the sixth rung, its
predictions too; every number quoted per seed against a paired floor; and version 1.0 defined by an
acceptance test and nothing else. \textbf{Monet v1.0
does not exist at the shipped vector.} The shipped bot reads $41.31\%$ on twelve seeds and
$40.11\%$ on twelve others ($14{,}400$ games each), against a bar of $50.0\%$; it beats their v0.2
and v0.3 on every seed and loses to their v0.4, v0.5 and v0.6 on every seed; and a second adapter,
written by an independent agent from FishLab's two protocol documents alone, reproduces the win rate
to the hundredth on twelve of twelve seeds and the game records byte for byte on ten. Three
mechanisms shipped on the way: a calibrated per-card marginal with a joint chain over it and a
licence term at low weight, a credit in the ask ranker for asks a hit-maximiser never plays, and a
translation of the rule set's declare compulsion that the bridge had been answering wrongly.
Ten other rungs were measured and shipped nothing, each inside its floor; the belief axis
closed on records studies rather than cells; the ask ranker closed on four fitted terms. The
finding the read could not have predicted is where the gains went: against their v0.4, v0.5,
v0.6 and SESTINA alike the ladder gained the same thirteen points, and none of the four is beaten
--- the wall at v0.4 stands where the first paper placed it. An attribution study locates the
frontier's margin --- $40\%$ of its extra sets in evenly dealt half-suits, $31\%$ in steals from
Monet's majorities --- and finds that at SESTINA's own decisions Monet's pick would hit $62.5\%$
against SESTINA's $55.7\%$: the hit chance is not the value of an ask. We report the ladder, the
table, the instrument and its two disclosed defects, and the negative result as the deliverable.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A laboratory that publishes a loss owes its readers the attempt. The first cross-engine measurement
of this project \cite{frontier} placed FishAI's agent between FishLab's v0.3 and v0.4 --- a release
four versions before search existed in that lineage --- and left ${\sim}16$ of the $22.92$ points to
even unattributed. The roadmap that followed \cite{monet} opened a new agent line, Monet, whose
stated goal is one sentence: \emph{Monet v1.0 beats SESTINA v1.0.} It then did something this
project had not done before at that scale: it wrote the acceptance test for v1.0 before the first
rung ran (six conditions, \S\ref{sec:table}), it wrote a prediction for every rung before that
rung's cells were played, and it recorded every rung whether or not anything shipped from it.
Seventeen rungs later the acceptance test has been executed in full on the shipped bot, and this
paper is its report.

The result is negative and the paper says so in the first line. The interest is in three other
things. First, \emph{what the fourteen points that were bought are made of} --- three shipped
mechanisms, one of which is not a policy change at all but a correction to how the bridge
translates a rule the two engines state differently. Second, \emph{why ten further rungs
bought nothing}, which is the record of a belief programme measured to its end: the inference was
studied on full-information records rather than cells, and every route into the belief that the
records could price was priced at zero or below. Third, \emph{where the ladder's gains went}.
Against FishLab's non-searching v0.4, v0.5 and v0.6 and against SESTINA the ladder gained the same
thirteen points, which says the shipped gains are worth the same against every opponent above the
wall and bought no ground on the wall itself. The frontier's margin is elsewhere, and the
attribution study of \S\ref{sec:sets} says where.

Everything in this paper is a number in \monet{} \cite{monet}, cited by section; the paper adds no
measurement of its own. FishLab's repository carries no licence file, and nothing of theirs is
reproduced here: their engine's game output is data, their documented guest-bot protocol is
implemented, and their measured findings are cited as theirs.

\section{The instrument}
\label{sec:instrument}

\paragraph{The bridge.} FishAI's decision function is registered as a guest bot in FishLab's C++
engine over their JSON-line protocol, as in \cite{frontier}. A cell is $200$ deals $\times$ $6$
seat rotations $= 1{,}200$ games on one seed; a read is twelve seeds, $14{,}400$ games, quoted as a
mean over seeds with its standard deviation and the paired standard error, against a paired floor of
$\pm 2.00$ points that the roadmap fixed before any rung (\monet{} \S6.3). Every cell of the ladder
is on the record with its seed, and every seed reported.

\paragraph{The controls.} The mirror cell is not one of them --- a policy against itself on
duplicate deals is forced to $50\%$ before a card is dealt, which \cite{frontier} learned at the cost
of a published wrong number. What is mandatory instead (\monet{} \S6.2): a per-process count of
every protocol op, cross-checked against the engine's own count of the guest's asks; a byte-exact
null arm, the mechanism switched off, reproducing a known cell line for line before any new seed is
spent; paired deals throughout; fault counters read by name; a calibration harness on every cell;
and a completion check, because cells have died silently on handshake timeouts. From v0.14 every arm
is also pinned \emph{in-engine}: the recorded play is replayed through the policy at home and must
agree with the bridge at $100.0\%$ of the arm's decisions before its number is read.

\paragraph{The rule set, translated.} \usff{} compels a cardless turn-holder to declare
\cite{rules}; FishLab's engine offers it an optional poll instead, and its own ladder resolves what
a guest declines. The bridge of \cite{frontier} answered the compulsion into the optional poll, and
the roadmap's records study of the declare (\monet{} \S3.8f) found that every such answer below
certainty spent a set to avoid a move that costs nothing. The translation --- a compelled claim whose
plan is not certain is answered \emph{none}, and the host's ladder takes it --- is worth $+1.28$
points paired on twelve seeds (SD $0.29$, SE $0.08$, ahead on all twelve). It is not a policy change
and it is not credited to any rung; it is the bridge reading the rules correctly. Numbers measured
before it and after it are on different bridges, and this paper never subtracts one from the other.

\paragraph{The records.} FishLab's engine keeps no game trace. A counters-only fork of their
\code{match} binary, extended with one flag, appends the deal and the event trace of each game as a
line of JSON after the game ends --- ground truth read after the fact, changing no branch a policy
can see, and controlled by requiring its whole cell output to equal the unmodified fork's on the
same cell (identical, at seed 90210). A loader at home reconstructs every seat's view from the deal
and the public log, checks every event against the engine's own hand counts and every declare
against its half-suit awards, and replays the policy at every ask decision of both sides: the
counterfactual agrees with the bridge play at $100.0\%$ of Monet's $102{,}000$ asks on the study's
records, and at $100\%$ on both sides of a mirror cell in their engine. That agreement is what
turns the records into an instrument: the same policy can be asked what it would have done at the
\emph{opponent's} decisions, scored on the true deal.

\paragraph{The second arm.} Condition 6 of the acceptance test requires the result to be
reproduced by an independently built adapter on the same specification. An agent that had never
seen this project's adapter was given FishLab's \code{BOT\_PACKAGE.md} and \code{PLAY.md} and a
one-page specification --- the policy, the decision-seed rule, the compulsion translation --- and
built one. Checked at home it agreed with the engine's own view constructor on $87{,}575$ views with
zero differences and on $16{,}449$ of $16{,}449$ decisions. Abroad it reproduces the first adapter's
win rate to the hundredth on all twelve seeds and its game records byte for byte on ten; the three
games in $28{,}800$ that differ are located in \S\ref{sec:table}.

\paragraph{What shipped.} The belief: a calibrated marginal over the unseen cards
(\code{pModel: 'marginal'}), a chain that places a set's open cards jointly over it when a declare is
planned (\code{pAssignment: 'joint'}), and a licence term that conditions the hit probability on
what a seat's own asks have revealed, at a weight the 24-seed cell decided (\code{licenceLambda}
$0.3$; $0.6$ read $+1.88$ over v0.4b on 22 of 24 seeds, and the pre-registered $0.3$-against-$0.6$
confirmation read $+1.19$, SE $0.30$, 19 of 24). Then the ask: a credit on the miss branch of the
ask ranker (\code{contest: 0.6}) for asks into sets the opponents hold most of --- the asks a
hit-maximiser never plays --- which is the one rung after v0.4c to clear the floor abroad,
$+4.04$ paired on twelve pre-registered seeds and ahead on every one. Then the bridge's translation
of the compulsion. Everything else on the vector is v0.1's Punter at \code{hard}.

\paragraph{What was measured and did not ship.} Opponent reading through the prior ($+0.47$),
communication (flat at home, never abroad), search at the affordable budget ($+0.14$ abroad; the
correct posterior does not make a 100 ms search pay in either leaf), the determinized declare
($+0.08$), the exposure charge ($+1.07$, two standard errors above zero and two under the floor:
real, and half a rung), the closing ask ($+0.58$, the same shape), the chase appetite (negative at
every dose, closed on the fit without spending the confirmation seeds), and the calibrated licence
likelihood ($-0.74$). Two more rungs were studies on the records that reached a verdict without a
cell (\S\ref{sec:belief}). Each of the ten is on the record with the prediction written before it
and the prediction's score after.

\paragraph{The rule that did the work.} A rung ships if its paired main effect clears $+2.00$ on
twelve fresh seeds; inside the floor with its marker moved, it is recorded and not shipped. Four
ranker terms were fitted in sequence on the same vector against a measured bound of $+5.8$ points
(the counterfactual surplus of \S\ref{sec:sets}); the first took $+4.04$ of it, the next three
$+1.07$, $+0.58$ and less than zero. The roadmap's own reading is that the ask ranker was finished
by v0.13 and that the last term was the informative one: it went looking for the missing chases,
found that a seat cannot identify them from its view, and paid a hundred points a decision to learn
it (\monet{} \S3.8i).

\section{The acceptance test}
\label{sec:table}

Monet v1.0 exists when, and only when (\monet{} \S3.9): (1) its win rate against SESTINA v1.0 is at
least $50.0\%$ pooled over twelve seeds of $200$ deals at the $\pm 2.00$ floor --- never on three
seeds, never on one cell; (2) every seed is reported and the SD across them published; (3) the panel
is monotone and Monet beats FishLab's v0.2 through v0.6 as well, because a bot that beats SESTINA and
loses to v0.5 has been fitted, not built; (4) declare accuracy is at least $98.0\%$ and every fault
counter zero; (5) every control of \S\ref{sec:instrument} passes, the mirror not among them; (6) the
result is reproduced by a second, independently built arm. The read was pre-registered on
2026-09-06 with six predictions and executed the same day (\monet{} \S3.8m).

$41.31\%$ and $40.11\%$, $1.20$ points apart with a standard error of $0.58$ between them --- two

\paragraph{Condition 4.} Declare accuracy on the 73 cells of the read runs $99.20$--$99.82\%$
against a bar of $98.0\%$; the parity with the frontier that \cite{frontier} found still holds.
The fourteen fault counters are zero on every cell played by the adapter as restated for this read
(\S\ref{sec:method}), and the engine's own \code{limit hits} line reads $0\%$ everywhere.

\paragraph{Condition 5.} The per-process count of the guest's asks equals the engine's own count
of them on 73 of 73 cells --- the cross-check the controls ask for, exact --- and the pass op is
exercised on every cell, which is the tripwire that would have caught the defect of \cite{frontier}
in an hour. The null arm, replayed on the identity seed before any new cell, reproduced the base
cell line for line. Calibration, the guest's own asks believed against realised: aggregate bias
$0.0046$--$0.0096$ against a bar of $0.01$ on twelve of twelve cells, worst decile $0.065$--$0.098$
against $0.10$ on twelve of twelve --- with the same shape the roadmap first measured at v0.3, the
belief a little too sure of its long shots and not sure enough of its good ones.

\paragraph{Condition 6, and the three games.} The independent adapter's twelve cells match the
first adapter's win rate on every seed and its records byte for byte on ten. On the other two, three
games of $28{,}800$ differ, each at one event, and each at the same position: a seat has just
declared its last cards, holds the turn with a pass pending, and the host polls the table. The first
adapter's cardless seat passes and the next teammate polled declares the team's next certain set;
the second arm's seat declares it itself before passing. Same set, same side, same final score.
\usff{}'s own reducer closes the declare window for the duration of a pending pass \cite{rules}, so
this is a position FishAI's rules never reach and the specification did not name; the two authors
resolved it differently, and the two resolutions differ on three events in $28{,}800$ games and on
no result. The condition is met, and the position is now specified.

\section{Where the sets go}
\label{sec:sets}

Before v0.9 the roadmap ran a study rather than a rung (\monet{} \S3.8c): full-information records of
Monet v0.4c against SESTINA on twelve seeds, split four ways by one instrument, with the next rung
to be chosen by a rule written before the tables were read. Monet won $34.28\%$ of those games and
$3.22$ sets a game to SESTINA's $4.25$, a gap of $1.033$ sets (SE $0.021$).

\paragraph{By the deal.} Split each half-suit by how many of its six cards Monet's side was dealt,
the set gap is $+0.608$ a game on Monet's majorities (four to six cards), $-0.414$ on the evenly
dealt threes, and $-1.227$ on SESTINA's majorities. Of SESTINA's $1.09$ extra \emph{cashed} sets a
game, the even half-suits carry $0.43$ ($40\%$; a $3$--$3$ deal is won by SESTINA 58 to 42),
SESTINA's steals from Monet's majorities $0.34$ ($31\%$), and its better conversion of its own
majorities $0.31$ ($29\%$). Gifts --- sets handed over by a wrong declare --- run the other way and
are small. At the clinch Monet holds $0.30$ sets a game locked and uncashed to SESTINA's $0.10$, and
holds a lock $7.84$ events to SESTINA's $4.34$: the game ends while Monet is still collecting, which
is the proof-latency finding of \cite{frontier} again, now bounded at about $0.2$ sets a game.

\paragraph{The counterfactual, and the third case.} At every ask decision of SESTINA the records
were replayed through Monet's policy, from SESTINA's hand and the public log, and scored on the true
deal beside the ask SESTINA actually made. The study's pre-registration named three cases and the
tables returned the third, decisively: Monet's pick would hit $62.5\%$ where SESTINA's asks hit
$55.7\%$ --- a surplus of $6.81$ points, SE $0.08$ over twelve seeds, the same on every seed and in
every holding bucket, and present in SESTINA's own mirror ($54.7$ against $49.4$). SESTINA agrees
with Monet's pick $42.8\%$ of the time, and where the two differ SESTINA's ask hits $41.1\%$ to the
pick's $53.0\%$. SESTINA leaves a quarter of the available hit chance on the table at every decision
and wins two games in three. \textbf{The hit chance is not the value of an ask.} What SESTINA's asks
buy shows in the same tables: its positions are richer --- the same Monet policy hits $52.4\%$ at
Monet's own positions and $62.5\%$ at SESTINA's --- and they get richer as the game goes, where
Monet's are flat. A hit publishes a card's location to the whole table and a miss publishes a
licence; SESTINA prices both, and Monet's ranker priced neither.

\paragraph{What v0.9 bought, and what it paid.} The contest credit moves $15.2\%$ of Monet's asks,
at the decisions where the greedy pick is poorest: there v0.4c's pick would hit $22.4\%$ and the
priced ask hits $24.4\%$ --- not worse asks, but asks placed elsewhere, into sets where Monet holds
one to three cards and away from the five-card closing asks that on a miss hand the turn over with
the set exposed. Monet keeps the turn longer ($41.7$ asks a game from $39.2$), the even half-suits
go to Monet $46.1\%$ of the time from $42.0\%$, its four-card majorities convert $62.1\%$ from
$57.2\%$, and the lock hold falls from $7.90$ events to $5.93$ because the run that formed the lock
is still going. It also pays what the study said a hit costs, and more: its hits are taken back
$43.4\%$ of the time from $41.0\%$, and its misses are made at more dangerous targets. Two of the
five markers the pre-registration wrote came in as written; the mechanism that won was the contest,
not the take-back the study had pointed at, and the record says so. FishLab's own finding about
SESTINA --- that ask accuracy is anti-correlated with strength near the optimum \cite{fishlab} ---
is confirmed in kind: Monet's accuracy \emph{rose} under the credit, because the decisions it changed
were the poorest ones.

\section{Why the belief axis closed}
\label{sec:belief}

The ladder's last three rungs, v0.14 to v0.16, were about the belief --- what Monet thinks each
seat holds --- and the first two of them never needed a cell. With the records of \S\ref{sec:sets}
and a seam that runs the real
policy on an injected belief with nothing in the engine touched, a candidate belief can be scored
against the true deal at every decision Monet made, and a change that does not move the belief where
the belief is wrong is dead before it plays a game.

\paragraph{The assignment (v0.14).} Is the side assignment --- which team holds a missing card ---
the binding constraint? Studied on two twelve-seed corpora of records (\monet{}
\S3.8j): the assignment is calibrated, the exact pricer that must first reproduce known answers
fails to, and the ceiling of the whole channel under a \emph{perfect} belief is $0.33$ sets a game,
half a point of win rate at the class prior. The axis closed on its calibration, and the candidate
that survived was a correctness fix to the deduction.

\paragraph{The deduction (v0.15).} The fix was studied before any code: exact conditioning on the
model's own constraints, in place of the approximation the engine ships. It reads \emph{worse} on the
records --- the pooled Brier score rises rather than falls --- and the pre-registration was wrong
about the direction of nearly everything it predicted (\monet{} \S3.8k). The model, not the
inference, is the error: a seat that has asked into a set holds about $1.5$ of its alive cards
whatever their number, and the model's licence carries no such likelihood.

\paragraph{The licence likelihood (v0.16).} So the likelihood was calibrated on the records --- three
knob families through the same seam, fit on three seeds and held out on twenty-one --- and the one
that survived its falsifiers repaired the belief exactly where v0.15 had located the error. Abroad it
lost: $40.58\%$ against the base's $41.31\%$, $-0.74$ paired, inside the floor, behind on sets on
ten of twelve seeds with its wrong declares doubled ($0.0125$ to $0.0239$ a game, worse on every
seed, seven standard errors). The loss is located and it is a lesson about instruments rather than
beliefs. The declare places a set's open cards by a chain over the same table, and that path was
never among the falsifiers, which all read pooled calibration; on the same declare windows the
repaired table prices one-guess plans above $0.9$ nineteen times as often as the shipped one, and
they come right $43\%$ of the time against the shipped table's $70\%$. \textbf{A pooled Brier score
cannot see a selected population.} The declare gate selects exactly the cells where a table is
over-confident, and no belief change on this ladder may now ship without the plan's reliability
read through the planner itself on the same windows.

\paragraph{The count.} Seven consecutive measured negatives, v0.10 through v0.16, the last with the
belief repaired where the error was and worth nothing in play, closed the axis (\monet{} \S3.8l). An
oracle arm that shares its team's true hands and cashes every lock the instant it exists bounded
every belief mechanism at $38.28\%$ on the v0.4-era ask policy (\monet{} \S3.4a); v0.9 reads above
it because the ask policy changed, not because the bound was wrong.

\section{Method notes}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Pre-registration, scored.} Every rung's section in \cite{monet} carries a
pre-registration written before its cells and a record that scores the predictions after. The scores
are reported whether or not they flatter the rung: v0.9's mechanism was not the one its markers
pointed at (two of five as written); v0.15's pre-registration missed on the sign of its headline;
v0.17's six predictions hit five and missed the sixth by a tenth of a point on one cell. The
acceptance read predicted that its own condition 6 would be met on the number and might differ in
the letter at a declare-window position, and it did, at a pending pass.

\paragraph{A stale instrument, disclosed.} The bridge adapter carries a cross-check that restates
each declare's card placement locally and counts a mismatch with the engine's own. It had restated
by the capacity-greedy rule since v0.1, and the engine has placed cards by the joint chain since
v0.4b, so the counter read non-zero on every kept log from v0.10 on --- $220$ to $459$ a cell against
about $11{,}000$ declares --- and was read as noise. It was a stale instrument, not a bot fault; the
wire always carried the engine's own placement. For the acceptance read the check was restated
through the engine's planner, the greedy comparison kept as information, and the restated adapter
replayed the identity cell byte-identical to the original: on that cell the information counter
reads $360$ and $723$, exactly the stale counts. The disclosure is in the record, and every record
from here reports the fourteen counters by name.

\paragraph{Where the counts come from.} The op counts are summed from per-process files, never from
the shared log, which loses about one process in thirty-six; the read's own pre-registration had
quoted bands from the log and its record restates them from the files, where the ask count equals the
engine's on every cell. The controls of \cite{frontier} were written from exactly this failure.

\paragraph{Cost.} Monet's policy decides an ask in about $0.14$ ms and a six-seat game in about
$82$ ms at home. The search arm at its pre-registered form costs $96$ ms an ask decision against a
budget of $100$; its wider form twice the budget and its deeper form $1.7$ times, and both were out on
the cost rule whatever they read ($-0.08$ and $+0.60$ sets a pair at home, SE about $0.34$).

\section{What is left}
\label{sec:left}

\paragraph{The residual is position.} The decomposition of \cite{frontier} --- Monet picks better
moves than the frontier at matched positions ($+5.49$) and arrives at worse ones ($-10.38$) --- was
measured on the defective bridge and has not been re-recorded, and the roadmap labels it a
hypothesis. Everything this ladder measured is consistent with it. The belief is calibrated and
repairing it buys nothing; the ask ranker is finished at four terms; a hit-maximising policy at the
frontier's own decisions would out-hit the frontier by seven points and the frontier wins anyway.
What the frontier buys with its asks is a richer position three moves later, and that is not a
knob on a one-step ranker.

\paragraph{Search at the affordable budget is a null.} The one channel with a ceiling above the
gap is lookahead, and at $100$ ms an ask the correct posterior does not make it pay ($+0.14$ abroad).
At that horizon the set differential almost never moves --- nine candidate means in ten read exactly
zero --- and a locked set counted as a set does not change what the ranker's own alternatives are
worth. What a search that pays would need is a leaf that prices what an ask publishes, which is the
same object \S\ref{sec:sets} found the frontier pricing and the ranker not.

\paragraph{The even half-suits.} $40\%$ of the frontier's margin sits in the $3$--$3$ deals, which
v0.9 moved from $42.0\%$ to $46.1\%$ and no rung has attacked as a target. It remains the leading
candidate for a rung, at the prior of a ranker term, which on this record is under the floor.

\paragraph{The decision.} The roadmap wrote the fork for this outcome before the first rung: ship the
fast policy in the browser at its honest maximum and publish the negative result, or fund a lab-only
search arm beyond the affordable budget. The owner took the first on 2026-09-06: v0.9 is what the
site's table seats, this paper is the artefact, and the direction from here is that a rung is chosen
by what it is measured to add to the number, with the same protocol --- pre-registered, paired,
floored, reproduced --- deciding what that is.

\section{Threats to validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\emph{One opponent.} The target is one frozen specification of one project's bot; nothing here is a
claim about Canadian Fish in general, and the panel exists to show that the gains are not a fit to
that one opponent --- they are the same size against four. \emph{One rule set.} Every number is
conditional on \usff{} as translated into a host that plays nine half-suits to the end where \usff{}
clinches at five; every figure here is read over the pre-clinch record, and the host's
\code{win-condition} warnings on every cell are the expected consequence of that difference.
\emph{Two bridges.} The compulsion translation moved every Monet number by about $+1.3$ points;
Table~\ref{tab:ladder} labels each row's bridge and the paper subtracts across the seam nowhere
except in the panel's inherited row, which the roadmap quotes as a difference of levels.
\emph{The second arm is a second adapter, not a second engine.} Condition 6 reproduces the bridge,
which is what it was written to do; the engine is FishLab's on both sides. \emph{The position
decomposition is on the defective bridge} and is a hypothesis, labelled as one. \emph{Statistical
resolution.} Twelve seeds resolve $\pm 2.00$ paired; two of the rungs that shipped nothing read
between one and two points with standard errors of a quarter to half a point, which is a real effect
under the floor, and the record says so rather than rounding either way.
