\title{FishAI v1.0: When Best-Response Adaptation\\ Is Worth Less Than Nothing}
\author{Allen Hsieh\\[2pt] {\small FishAI project}}
\date{August 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
FishAI v0.5 measured a nine-style roster for the \usff{} dialect of Literature (Canadian
Fish) and found one style, Punter, dominant. FishAI v1.0 asks the natural follow-up: does
an agent that identifies its opponents' styles online and best-responds beat committing to
the best static style? The agent is built honestly --- fourteen behavioural features
certified by the public log alone, calibrated diagonal-Gaussian posteriors per opponent
seat, best response over the measured $9 \times 9$ counter table, delegation to the
unchanged style-conditioned engine, all under determinism and statelessness constraints
that force co-located seats to identical reads. The answer is a theorem plus a
measurement. The theorem: the Punter row of the measured table dominates every column, and
expected payoff is linear in the opponent posterior, so the warm best response is Punter
under \emph{every} belief --- the adaptive policy provably degenerates to the dominant
static style. The measurement: the degenerate policy is strictly \emph{worse} than static
Punter, because its warmup anchor pays a tax. The adaptive team lands below the paired
Punter benchmark in all nine gauntlet cells (mean $-0.0108$; sharpest cell
$-0.0108 \pm 0.0031$, $z = -3.53$) and loses a truly-paired 24-composition mixed screen at
$-0.0136 \pm 0.0043$ ($z = -3.2$, seed-clustered). An oracle ablation prices perfect
classification at
exactly zero: handed the true styles, the agent plays move-for-move identical games. The
classifier has real but bounded skill --- $22.4\%$ end-of-game top-1 against an $11.1\%$
chance floor, $50.4\%$ on Ghost, $5.6$--$12.9\%$ inside a quadrangle of styles that
diverge from the control on under $3\%$ of decisions --- and under dominance none of it
can matter. Adaptation over this roster is worth less than nothing, and every knob that
repairs the tax does so by collapsing the policy into the static dominant style. That is a
statement about this meta-game, not about adaptation as an idea, and we state which
conditions --- intransitive rosters, off-roster opponents, rule-set shifts --- would give
the machinery something to do.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The FishAI v0.5 tournament \cite{v05paper} ended with a static answer to a static
question: among nine parameterized play styles sharing one full-strength inference engine,
the completion-chasing Punter dominates. The obvious rejoinder, standard in every
community that argues about styles, is dynamic: \emph{a fixed style is a sitting target; a
player who reads the table and adapts should beat any static commitment.} FishAI v1.0 was
built to test that rejoinder under the same measurement discipline, with the question
fixed before any experiment ran: \emph{does adapting to the opponents' identified styles
beat committing to the best static style?}

The v1.0 agent is the natural best-response architecture, built without shortcuts: an
observation layer that extracts per-seat behavioural features the public log \emph{certifies}
rather than estimates (Section~\ref{sec:observe}); a classifier that turns those features
into a posterior over the nine roster styles, calibrated offline and honest about early-game
ignorance (Section~\ref{sec:classify}); a best-response layer that scores every candidate
style against the posterior using the measured $9 \times 9$ counter table and delegates the
chosen style to the unchanged v0.5 engine (Section~\ref{sec:bestrespond}); and structural
honesty constraints --- public information only, bit-level determinism, and statelessness
via phase quantisation so co-located teammates reach identical reads
(Section~\ref{sec:honesty}).

The result is a negative one, and it arrives in an unusually clean two-part form. First,
\textbf{a one-paragraph theorem} (Section~\ref{sec:theorem}): the Punter row of the
measured counter table dominates every column, and the agent's expected payoff is linear
in its opponent posterior, so the warm best response is Punter under \emph{every} belief
--- uniform, oracle-exact, or anything the classifier can produce --- and the adaptive
policy provably degenerates to the dominant static style. Second, \textbf{a measured
warmup tax} (Section~\ref{sec:results}): degenerating to Punter would merely tie the
static benchmark, but the measured agent does worse, because its warmup anchor (Balanced,
played until the public log matures) is a below-Punter style played for $52$--$58\%$ of
the team's decisions. The tax is visible in all nine gauntlet cells (mean $-0.0108$),
significant in the sharpest ($z = -3.53$), and confirmed at $z = -3.2$ in a truly-paired
mixed-population screen. Two of the four pre-registered predictions --- written down,
verbatim, before the run --- expected the tax to be negligible; both were refuted, and
the refutation \emph{is} the headline.

The paper's contributions: the architecture as an honest reference implementation of
online style identification in Literature (Section~\ref{sec:arch}); the
dominance-degeneracy theorem and its corollary that perfect classification is worth exactly
zero, confirmed by an oracle ablation whose per-deal deltas are identically $0.0000$
(Sections~\ref{sec:theorem}, \ref{sec:oracle}); the pre-registered suite and its verdicts
(Sections~\ref{sec:design}--\ref{sec:results}); and a mechanism-level account of the
warmup tax, with the observation that every remedy collapses the adaptive policy into the
static dominant style --- not a bug in the remedies but the finding itself
(Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Opponent modelling --- fitting a model of another agent's policy or type and responding to
it --- is surveyed by Albrecht and Stone \cite{albrechtstone2018}. In trick-taking card
games, Rebstock et al.\ show that policy-based inference substantially improves state
estimation in Skat \cite{rebstock2019}; the project's internal research synthesis
\cite{playstyles} catalogues this family for Literature (its opponent-model exploiter,
A14) and specifies a per-seat detection panel (S35, ``the Reader'') whose feature set
anticipates the observation layer built here. This paper implements a deliberately narrow
slice of that program --- type identification over a known, closed roster --- and measures
what the slice is worth. The meta-game framing follows Balduzzi et al.\
\cite{balduzzi2018}: the v0.5 matrix is the evaluation data, its maximum-entropy Nash
places all mass on Punter, and $\alpha$-Rank \cite{omidshafiei2019} concentrates likewise,
so a best-response dynamic over that matrix has a unique fixed point; this paper measures
the cost of travelling to it. The self-play caveat of Hu et al.\ \cite{hu2020} ---
behaviour optimised within a closed population need not transfer outside it --- is
inherited in Section~\ref{sec:threats}. To our knowledge, resting on the synthesis's
negative search report \cite{playstyles,v05paper}, no prior quantitative study of online
style adaptation exists for Literature.

\section{Background: the game, the dialect, and the v0.5 roster}
\label{sec:background}

Literature is a six-player, two-team card game of public asks and hidden hands. Under the
\usff{} dialect --- specified rule-complete in the rules document \cite{rules} and
summarized in the companion paper \cite{v05paper} --- the deck is 54 cards in nine
half-suits (``sets'' or ``books'') of six; each seat is dealt nine; asks require holding a
card of the asked set and not the named card; declares happen out of turn in a
deterministic declare window; \emph{any} declaration error awards the set to the
opponents; the game ends the moment a team is awarded five sets; ties are impossible. The
engine is pure, deterministic TypeScript: one seed reproduces a byte-identical game.

FishAI v0.5 \cite{v05paper} defined nine parameterized styles over one shared
full-strength inference engine --- Balanced (the tuned control), Blitz and Punter
(aggressive), Banker and Turtle (conservative), Hoarder (optionality), and Scout, Ghost
and Archivist (information) --- and measured their full-precision round robin (36 cells
$\times$ 4{,}300 duplicate deal pairs; per-cell SE at most $0.005$) \cite{matrixv2}. The
verdict was \emph{dominant --- Punter} (mean $0.5829$,
maximin $0.5190$, cyclic energy $0.0112$, exploitability $0.0025$), with two measured
caveats that matter here: the declare-threshold axis the roster's labels advertise is
inert across the roster's entire range \cite{inertpaper}, so the nine styles differ from
the control on only $0.39$--$2.89\%$ of decisions \cite{styles}; and the Hoarder's
theorized benefit, the contained-book turn-pass, was implemented and measured to be worth
nothing \cite{containedpaper}. The first caveat returns in Section~\ref{sec:p4} as a
quantitative bound on what any public-log classifier can see.

\section{The adaptive architecture}
\label{sec:arch}

The v1.0 agent is a policy shape, not a new engine. At every decision it (i) observes the
three opponent seats from the public log, (ii) classifies each into a posterior over the
nine styles, (iii) best-responds by expected score rate over the measured counter table,
and (iv) delegates: the chosen style is handed to the ordinary v0.5 engine, which decides
exactly as for any static style at full strength \cite{repo}. Everything above the
delegation boundary is a pure function of the seat's public view and the configuration.

\subsection{Observation: features the public log certifies}
\label{sec:observe}

The observation layer is a single $O(\text{events})$ pass over the public log producing
one feature vector per seat. Its design rule is that every feature is an \emph{exact}
public observation, never an estimate: a hit locates the named card publicly at the asker
until it moves again; an ask certifies that the asker held a card of the asked set (the
only licence to ask) and proves they lacked the named card; a declaration's reveal lists
the true holder of all six cards, so foreign and own-hand-only declares are exact events;
and hand counts are \emph{replayed} from the log rather than read from the view, which is
what makes truncated-log evaluation honest --- a prefix's replayed counts are correct at
the truncation point while the view's end-of-game counts are not \cite{repo}.

One omission shapes everything downstream: \textbf{declining to declare emits no event}.
The declare window advances silently, so declare-window patience is invisible to any
public observer, and --- as Section~\ref{sec:warmupanatomy} measures --- the public log
grows several times slower than the decision count.

\subsection{Classification: calibrated diagonal-Gaussian fingerprints}
\label{sec:classify}

The classifier is deliberately the simplest model that can be honest: one diagonal
Gaussian per style per game-length bucket, calibrated offline from 150 \usff{} mirror
games per style and committed with full provenance \cite{artifact}. For an observed vector
$x$ and a style with per-feature mean $\mu$ and deviation $\sigma$, the score is the plain
z-distance $\sum_i -\tfrac12 z_i^2$, $z_i = (x_i - \mu_i)/\sigma_i$, softmaxed over the
nine styles. Four calibration decisions were made by measurement, not taste.
\emph{The Gaussian normaliser is dropped}: with sample SDs over a few hundred calibration
vectors, $\sum_i -\ln\sigma_i$ is a large constant bonus for whichever style calibrated
tightest --- in the first calibration it handed the Ghost 40 of 60 mirror-Balanced reads
--- and without it the argmax at a style's own mean is that style. \emph{Per-style
variance is shrunk halfway to the pooled variance},
$\sigma'^2 = (\sigma_{\text{style}}^2 + \sigma_{\text{pooled}}^2)/2$: raw per-style SDs
make the widest-variance style an outlier magnet, fully pooled SDs discard real
information, and the blend beat both ends in calibration probes. \emph{Checkpoint
buckets}: a mid-game observation must be read against fingerprints taken at the same
horizon, so the calibration stores buckets at $\{60, 120, 200, 300\}$ observed events plus
a full-game bucket. \emph{Sample-size damping}: the posterior is blended toward uniform by
$\min(1, \text{asks}/12)$ --- at zero observed asks the answer is exactly ``I don't
know'', and full confidence requires a dozen of the seat's \emph{own} choices, not merely
a long game. Determinism is absolute: the same view and fingerprints produce identical
output, ties resolving to the earlier fingerprint in roster order.

\subsection{Best response over the measured counter table}
\label{sec:bestrespond}

The payoffs are the committed counter table $P$: $P_{ij}$ is the duplicate-averaged score
rate of style $i$ against style $j$ from the v0.5 full-precision matrix (matrix v2;
diagonal $0.5$ by duplicate symmetry) \cite{matrixv2}, reproduced in
Table~\ref{tab:counter}. Provenance travels with the table --- the source artifact's
digest is exported beside the numbers, pinned by tests, and enforced by the experiment
harness (Section~\ref{sec:provenance}).

For opponent-seat posteriors $q_1, q_2, q_3$ over the styles, the agent scores every
candidate row $i$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:expected}
E(i) \;=\; \frac{1}{3}\sum_{k=1}^{3}\;\sum_{s} q_k(s)\, P_{is}
\;=\; \sum_{s} \bar q(s)\, P_{is},
\qquad \bar q = \tfrac{1}{3}(q_1 + q_2 + q_3),
\end{equation}
adds a hysteresis bias $\beta$ (\code{switchMargin}, default $0.01$) to the anchor
style's entry --- a candidate must \emph{beat} the anchor, not merely tie it --- and
plays the argmax. During \emph{warmup} (fewer than \code{warmupEvents} $= 40$ observed
events) the anchor (default Balanced, the control) plays unconditionally: an early
posterior is mostly the damping prior, and a best response to ignorance is noise.

\subsection{Honesty constraints}
\label{sec:honesty}

Three constraints are structural, inherited from v0.5 and extended. \emph{Public
information only}: the agent consumes \code{SeatView}, the exact public payload a human in
the seat would see; no other hand is reachable by construction \cite{v05paper,repo}.
\emph{Determinism}: the style choice is a pure function of (view, configuration) --- no
clock, no randomness, no state between calls --- so every reported game is reproducible
from its seed. \emph{Statelessness, via phase quantisation}: a stateful bot would remember
its last choice and demand a margin to move off it; this bot owns no state, because two
adaptive seats given the same public information \emph{must} reach the same read --- the
roster's one-shared-engine discipline \cite{styles} would otherwise die at the adaptive
layer. Hysteresis is therefore expressed statelessly, twice over: the memoryless anchor
bias $\beta$, and \emph{phase quantisation} --- the choice may only change when
$\lfloor \text{events}/30 \rfloor$ changes. In the implementation's own words: the
posterior ``is evaluated on the log cut to the last multiple of [30] events, so every view
inside one phase --- and every seat looking at it --- evaluates the identical prefix and
reaches the identical choice. A seat therefore cannot flip styles mid-phase, which is the
behavioural point of hysteresis, without a single byte of remembered state'' \cite{repo}.
The warmup gate reads the same truncation, so the whole selection is a function of the
phase alone; with the defaults the first warm phase begins at 60 observed events --- the
first multiple of 30 at or above 40.

\section{The dominance-degeneracy theorem}
\label{sec:theorem}

One look at Table~\ref{tab:counter} gives the game away: the Punter row is the
column-wise maximum everywhere. What that implies for the adaptive policy is one
paragraph of linear algebra.

\begin{theorem}[Dominance degeneracy]
\label{thm:dominance}
Let $P \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ be the counter table and suppose row $r$ weakly
dominates every row: $P_{rs} \ge P_{is}$ for all styles $i$ and $s$. Then for every triple
of opponent posteriors $(q_1, q_2, q_3)$, $r$ maximizes the expected payoff
$E(\cdot)$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:expected}; if the dominance is strict in every column, the
argmax is unique. Moreover, with the anchor bias --- $E(a)$ replaced by $E(a) + \beta$ for
the anchor $a \ne r$ --- $r$ remains the unique argmax whenever
$\min_s\, (P_{rs} - P_{as}) > \beta$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proofenv}
By Eq.~\eqref{eq:expected}, $E$ is linear in the mean posterior $\bar q$, which lies in
the probability simplex. For any $i$,
$E(r) - E(i) = \sum_s \bar q(s)\,(P_{rs} - P_{is})$ is a convex combination of
non-negative numbers, hence $\ge 0$; if every difference is strictly positive it is
$> 0$, since $\bar q$ places mass somewhere. For the anchor,
$E(r) - \big(E(a) + \beta\big) = \sum_s \bar q(s)\,(P_{rs} - P_{as}) - \beta
\ge \min_s (P_{rs} - P_{as}) - \beta > 0$.
\end{proofenv}

\noindent
The measured table instantiates the strict case: Punter's smallest margin over the best
rival row is $0.0112$ (Blitz row, Hoarder column), and its smallest margin over the
Balanced anchor row is $0.0171$ --- above the default $\beta = 0.01$, so not even the
anchor bias can rescue another choice. Hence:

\begin{corollary}[The warm policy is static]
\label{cor:static}
Under the committed table and defaults, the warm adaptive choice is Punter at every
decision, under every posterior the classifier can produce and every oracle assignment.
The adaptive policy is exactly: Balanced for the warmup phases, Punter thereafter.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Perfect classification is worth exactly zero]
\label{cor:oracle}
Classifier and oracle modes share the warmup and phase rules and differ only in the
posterior. By Corollary~\ref{cor:static} they delegate identically at every decision, so
on the same seed they produce byte-identical games: the oracle ablation's per-deal delta
is identically zero --- not approximately, exactly.
\end{corollary}

One statistical qualification, stated rather than buried. Dominance here is a property of
the \emph{committed point-estimate table}, which is the object the engine consults. As a
claim about the true matrix it is strongly but not perfectly supported: of the 72
row-vs.-Punter-row comparisons (8 rival rows $\times$ 9 columns), 70 exceed even the
Bonferroni-corrected two-sided bound $|z| > 2.773$ under a conservative independent
combination of the paired cell SEs; the weakest, the Blitz row in the Hoarder column
($0.0112$), sits at $z = 1.86$. If the true matrix inverted that margin, the argmax could
depend on beliefs concentrated on the Hoarder --- and the value at stake would be bounded
by roughly $0.011$ in score rate. The implementation anticipates this honestly: its tests
re-derive the argmax from the table rather than hard-coding Punter, so a future table with
an intransitive cycle flows through the same code and simply makes the adaptation
non-trivial \cite{repo}.

\section{Experimental design}
\label{sec:design}

\subsection{Pre-registration}
\label{sec:prereg}

The committed counter table already implied Theorem~\ref{thm:dominance}; the experiments
check the implication against play, not discover it. Four predictions were written into
the suite's source before the run and travel verbatim inside the artifact \cite{artifact}:

\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbf{P1}] The counter table's Punter row weakly dominates every column, so a warm
  adaptive team should match Punter's matrix-v2 row against every pure style within CI.
  Any detectable shortfall is the price of the warmup anchor (Balanced until ${\sim}60$
  observed events).
\item[\textbf{P2}] The adaptive-vs-Punter mixed-population delta should be ${\approx}\,0$:
  both teams best-respond to every composition with the same style once warm, so
  adaptation buys nothing a fixed Punter team does not already have.
\item[\textbf{P3}] Oracle classification should add ${\approx}$ nothing: the best response
  is Punter regardless of the read, so a perfect read and a classifier read select the
  same play.
\item[\textbf{P4}] Classifier top-1 accuracy should be good for Turtle, Ghost and Hoarder,
  and heavily confused inside the balanced/blitz/punter/banker quadrangle (the
  separability read: those four diverge from Balanced on under $1.3\%$ of decisions
  \cite{styles}).
\end{itemize}

The verdict rules were committed alongside. P1 and P3 are nine simultaneous cells: a cell
\emph{rejects} when $|z|$ exceeds the Bonferroni-corrected two-sided $5\%$ bound of
$2.773$; any rejection refutes; cells between $1.96$ and the bound are named but do not
refute (with nine cells, ${\sim}0.45$ land there by chance). P2 is one truly-paired
comparison at $|z| \le 1.96$. P4 was operationalized as three conditions on the
end-of-game reads: mean top-1 over $\{$Turtle, Ghost, Hoarder$\}$ $\ge 0.50$; mean top-1
over the quadrangle $\le 0.35$; and, for every quadrangle style, at least half its errors
landing inside the quadrangle. A refuted prediction is emitted as \code{refuted}, not
massaged.

\subsection{The five experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

All experiments use duplicate deal pairs (each seeded deal played in both orientations,
start seat rotating $i \bmod 6$), the paired estimator, and the v0.5 health discipline
\cite{botlab,v05paper}. The full suite is 125{,}600 games \cite{artifact}:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Gauntlet} --- the adaptive team against each of the nine pure styles, at
  4{,}300 pairs per cell on \emph{matrix v2's exact seed list} (prefix \code{style-v1}),
  both orientations, same start seats. That choice is the experiment: every deal behind
  Punter's committed row is replayed by the adaptive team, making the Punter row a
  per-deal benchmark (cross-run caveat below). Every gauntlet game also records which
  style each adaptive decision delegated to, split warmup/warm by the engine's own phase
  rule --- the recorded delegation \emph{is} what was played.
\item \textbf{Adaptive mirror} --- 400 pairs of adaptive-vs-adaptive. The policy is
  deterministic and identical on both teams, so the two orientations of a pair are
  literally the same game: the paired score must be exactly $0.5$ with SE exactly $0$. A
  plumbing symmetry check, and it passed ($0.5000 \pm 0.0000$ over 800 games).
\item \textbf{Mixed-population screen} --- 24 opponent compositions $\times$ 400 pairs on
  prefix \code{mixed-v1}, each played twice: the adaptive team, and a pure Punter team, on
  the same seeds, start seats and orientations. The headline is the pooled per-deal delta
  (adaptive $-$ Punter), \emph{truly paired within the run}; the harness verifies --- not
  assumes --- that both arms played identical (pair, orientation, seed, start-seat) sets.
  Because all 24 compositions replay the identical 400-seed list, the pooled SE is
  clustered by seed --- per-deal deltas are averaged within seed before the SE is taken
  over the 400 seed-level means --- so a deal's replays are never counted as independent
  evidence (per-composition SEs remain within-composition). The compositions are a fully
  specified deterministic sample: every 7th of the 165 lexicographic 3-multisets.
\item \textbf{Oracle ablation} --- the nine gauntlet cells re-run at 400 pairs with the
  true opponent styles handed to the adaptive seats. The classifier arm is not re-run: the
  gauntlet's first 400 pairs \emph{are} the classifier arm on identical seeds.
\item \textbf{Classifier accuracy} --- all 36 unordered style pairings, 50 single games
  each on prefix \code{clsacc-v1}. Each finished game's public log is truncated at
  $\{40, 80, 150, 250\}$ events (a checkpoint scores only games whose log outlived it)
  plus the full log; top-1 is scored per seat against the seat's true style.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Paired benchmarks and the cross-run caveat}
\label{sec:pairing}

The gauntlet's benchmark is Punter's row of the committed matrix-v2 artifact
\cite{matrixv2}, and the run's provenance block states the caveat verbatim
\cite{artifact}: ``Per-deal: the gauntlet replayed the benchmark seed list exactly (both
orientations, same start seats). The pairing is cross-run --- per-game records are not
joined --- so the delta SE is the conservative independent combination, an upper bound on
the true paired SE.'' The conservatism cuts one way: it makes the P1 test \emph{harder}
to refute, so the refutation reported below is understated, not manufactured. The mixed
screen and oracle ablation are paired within the run and carry no such caveat. One
gauntlet cell is sharper than the rest: against Punter itself the benchmark is exactly
$0.5$ with SE $0$ by duplicate symmetry.

\subsection{Health gates and provenance}
\label{sec:provenance}

A run is void unless its counters are clean; for the reported run \cite{artifact}:
\code{illegalActions} 0, \code{cappedGames} 0, \code{invariantViolations} 0, \code{ties}
0, \code{voids} 0, \code{nonClinch} 0, and \code{distinctSeeds} equal to the expected pair
count in every cell; the suite adds two pairing gates (the mixed arms' seed-set identity,
and the oracle cells' identity with the gauntlet head), both passed. The artifact is
committed at \code{src/lab/data/adaptive-results.json} with records digest
\code{ce3316641a8f38fe}, engine commit \code{45ec9f3} (recorded \code{45ec9f3-dirty}), and
the rules-document hash (\code{b4c7fef7\ldots}) shared with v0.5; the 125{,}600 games took
about $9.7$ minutes of wall clock. Because an adaptive result is only as honest as the
data it consulted, the artifact also carries the provenance of both committed calibrations
--- the counter table (from matrix v2, digest \code{0c3cb524a3534d4a}) and the
fingerprints (\code{gen-fingerprints}, prefix \code{fingerprint-v1}) --- and the assembly
step \emph{throws} if the benchmark's digest differs from the counter table's source
digest: the row the gauntlet is read against and the table the agent best-responded with
must come from the same matrix.

\subsection{The delegation record: the mechanism, observed}
\label{sec:usage}

The gauntlet instrumented every adaptive decision with the style it delegated to. The
record is total and unambiguous \cite{artifact}: in all nine cells, $100\%$ of warmup
decisions delegated to Balanced (the anchor) and $100\%$ of warm decisions delegated to
Punter --- not one of the $12.1$ million warm decisions across the gauntlet chose anything
else. This is Corollary~\ref{cor:static} observed in play, at every posterior the
classifier actually produced. The warmup phase accounted for $52.2\%$ (vs.\ Turtle) to
$58.2\%$ (vs.\ Blitz) of the adaptive team's decisions. Warm $=$ pure Punter plus warmup
$=$ a majority of decisions on a dominated style is the entire causal story of
Tables~\ref{tab:gauntlet} and~\ref{tab:mixed}, made quantitative next.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Anatomy of the warmup tax}
\label{sec:warmupanatomy}

Why does a 40-event warmup swallow half the game? Because the public log grows far more
slowly than the decision count: declining to declare emits no public event
(Section~\ref{sec:observe}), and declare-window declines dominate play. Gauntlet games
average $683$--$767$ engine moves, of which the adaptive team's three seats decide
$342$--$384$, yet across the 1{,}800 accuracy games --- whose lengths are of the same
scale --- not one public log reached 250 events and only $6.2\%$ reached 150
(Table~\ref{tab:clscurve}). The warm threshold of 60 observed events therefore lands past
the midpoint of the team's decisions: $52$--$58\%$ of them are taken by the anchor.

A deliberately crude model prices this: suppose a game's score rate were linear in the
mixture of styles played --- warmup share $w$ at the Balanced row's payoff, $(1-w)$ at
the Punter row's --- so the predicted shortfall against pure Punter is $-w g$, where $g$
is the Balanced deficit in that opponent's column of Table~\ref{tab:counter}. Table~\ref{tab:tax} runs this per cell: the zeroth-order
prediction lands within $0.8$ standard errors of the measured delta in all nine cells,
and the ordering is reproduced --- the worst measured cell (Ghost, $-0.0177$) is exactly
the column where the Balanced-vs-Punter gap is widest ($0.0319$). Nothing subtle is
happening: the tax is the anchor's known per-decision deficit times the fraction of
decisions the anchor takes; the mixed screen's $-0.0136$ sits in the same range, as a
deal-weighted average over 24 columns must.

\subsection{The obvious remedies, and why each one is the finding}
\label{sec:remedies}

Two one-line configuration changes eliminate the tax, and both are instructive to refuse.

\textbf{Set the anchor to Punter.} Then warmup plays Punter, the warm argmax is Punter
(Theorem~\ref{thm:dominance}, with the anchor bias now \emph{reinforcing} the dominant
row), and the ``adaptive'' policy is the static Punter with a classifier attached as
ornamentation. \textbf{Set warmup to zero.} Then the earliest posteriors are the damping
prior --- near-uniform --- and the argmax under uniform is still the dominant row: Punter
from move one. Intermediate tunings behave the same way: shortening warmup, shrinking the
phase, or raising the switch margin each moves the policy monotonically toward a static
endpoint (always-anchor or always-Punter), because under dominance there is no belief at
which the warm choice is anything else. Every knob that repairs the tax does so by
deleting the adaptation.

This is not a criticism of the knobs; it is the result. Over a dominance-solvable roster,
the best policy in the adaptive family is its degenerate member, and any non-degenerate
member pays for its flexibility with a measurable loss.

\subsection{What the classifier can and cannot see}
\label{sec:clslimits}

The classifier's limits connect quantitatively to the inert-axis result
\cite{inertpaper}. The decision-divergence instrument of v0.5 measured how often each
style's chosen action differs from the control's on identical views: Scout $2.89\%$,
Ghost $2.15\%$, Archivist $1.66\%$, Banker $1.19\%$, Turtle $0.83\%$, Punter $0.47\%$,
Hoarder $0.47\%$, Blitz $0.39\%$ \cite{styles}. A public-log classifier sees only the
accumulated consequences of divergent decisions, so styles that diverge on under $3\%$ of
decisions --- all of them --- leave thin trails, and the quadrangle styles, at under
$1.2\%$, leave almost none: their $0.056$--$0.129$ top-1 is the separability bound made
flesh. But divergence \emph{rate} is not the whole story: Hoarder and Turtle diverge
rarely yet classify at $0.31$ and $0.27$, their rare divergences concentrated in declare
events with loud public signatures (\code{foreignDeclareShare}, \code{ownHandOnlyShare},
\code{declareBackload}), while Archivist diverges three times as often yet classifies at
$0.118$, its trail absorbed by Scout's neighbouring fingerprint. What a behavioural
classifier reads is not how often a style is different but how \emph{loudly}, per event
the log deigns to record. None of this matters to the payoff ---
Section~\ref{sec:oracle} priced the whole apparatus at exactly zero against this roster
--- but it is the part of v1.0 that would survive a roster where classification did
matter; the measured accuracies calibrate what it could contribute.

\subsection{What would make adaptation valuable}
\label{sec:valuable}

The degeneracy is a property of the meta-game, not of the machinery, which was
deliberately built so that a different meta-game flows through unchanged (its tests
re-derive the argmax from the table rather than pinning Punter \cite{repo}). Three
departures would give the classifier's output a decision to influence:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{An intransitive roster.} Theorem~\ref{thm:dominance} needs a dominant row.
  A roster engineered with genuine counters --- which v0.5 measured this one not to be
  (cyclic energy $0.0112$) \cite{v05paper} --- makes the argmax belief-dependent, and then
  classifier accuracy and warmup cost become terms in a real trade-off.
\item \textbf{Off-roster opponents.} Humans at the project's play table are the actual
  target of the S35/A14 program \cite{playstyles}: they are not columns of any committed
  table, their tendencies drift, and exploiting them requires exactly the
  observe-classify-respond loop built here --- plus fingerprints calibrated from logged
  human play, a data-collection decision the synthesis flags as prior to everything else.
\item \textbf{Rule-set shifts.} The \usff{} gift rule is what prices Punter's risk
  appetite correctly \cite{v05paper}; under the 48-card baseline the declaration economics
  flip, the counter table would need re-measurement, and nothing guarantees the new table
  a dominant row.
\end{itemize}

Until one of those holds, the honest statement stands: over this roster, under these
rules, the best adaptive policy is the degenerate one, and every measured alternative is
worth less than nothing.

\section{Threats to validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Cross-run pairing (P1).} The gauntlet's delta SEs are conservative upper
  bounds (Section~\ref{sec:pairing}) --- which can only have hidden significance, not
  manufactured it; the truly-paired, seed-clustered P2 screen agrees at $z = -3.2$.
\item \textbf{Dominance is of the committed table.} The theorem consumes point estimates;
  70 of 72 dominance comparisons clear the corrected bound, but the weakest margin
  ($0.0112$, Blitz row, Hoarder column) is only $1.86$ conservative SEs from zero
  (Section~\ref{sec:theorem}). A true matrix inverting it would make the warm argmax
  belief-dependent in a narrow region, changing the result's form but not its economics
  (the value at stake is ${\lesssim}0.011$).
\item \textbf{Fingerprints are calibrated in mirror games.} Deployed reads happen in
  cross-play, where opponents shape game length and event mix. The checkpoint buckets and
  length-invariant features mitigate this, and the accuracy experiment itself measures on
  cross-play pairings, but the mismatch is real and untuned.
\item \textbf{Warmup and phase constants are design choices, not tuned values.}
  Section~\ref{sec:remedies} is why tuning was not pursued: under dominance every tuning
  direction terminates in a static policy, so a tuned v1.0 would measure only the speed
  of its own disappearance.
\item \textbf{Statelessness is a design constraint with alternatives.} A stateful agent
  could implement hysteresis with memory and act mid-phase; Theorem~\ref{thm:dominance}
  does not depend on the phase construction, but the measured warmup boundary (60 events)
  is an artifact of the stateless gate.
\item \textbf{Closed population.} All conclusions are self-play within one roster, one
  engine, one rule set (pinned by the artifact's rules hash); the transfer warning of
  \cite{hu2020} applies in full, and Section~\ref{sec:valuable} lists the exits. The
  classifier-accuracy numbers are additionally conditional on full-strength opponents.
  (The adaptive mirror's exact $0.5000 \pm 0.0000$ certifies harness symmetry only;
  correctness rests on the engine's audits \cite{v05paper,repo}.)
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

FishAI v1.0 built the adaptive agent the style debate keeps invoking --- observe the
table, identify the styles, counter them --- and measured what it is worth against the
measured meta-game. The answer has three layers. Adaptation over this roster
\emph{converges}: the counter table's Punter row dominates every column, expected payoff
is linear in belief, and the warm agent delegated every one of twelve million measured
warm decisions to Punter --- the theorem observed in play. Adaptation over this roster
\emph{costs}: the warmup anchor plays a dominated style for $52$--$58\%$ of decisions
because the public log, which records no declines, matures far more slowly than the game,
and the resulting tax --- $-0.0108$ mean across the gauntlet, $-0.0136 \pm 0.0043$ truly
paired in mixed company --- is reproduced within one standard error, in every cell, by
the crudest possible mixing model. And classification, the part of the architecture with
genuine measured skill ($22.4\%$ top-1 against $11.1\%$ chance, half a coin-flip on the
Ghost, structurally blind inside a quadrangle of styles that diverge on under $1.2\%$ of
decisions), is worth exactly zero: the oracle deltas are $0.0000$ to the last digit,
because under dominance no belief changes the play.

Two of four pre-registered predictions were refuted, both in the informative direction:
the project predicted convergence and got it, predicted the convergence would be free and
was billed for it. The billing is the contribution. ``Just adapt'' is, over this roster
and under these rules, strictly worse than picking the best static style and playing it
--- worth less than nothing --- and every configuration that repairs the loss does so by
quietly becoming that static style. That is a measured fact about this meta-game, not a
verdict on adaptation as an idea: an intransitive roster, an off-roster human, or a rule
shift would each hand the machinery a real decision, and the machinery is ready for the
first opponent that gives it one.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Reproducibility.} The engine is deterministic; the artifact
\cite{artifact} carries the seed prefixes, configuration, digests, commits, rules hash,
the verbatim predictions and both calibrations' provenance; every number here is in it,
the counter table \cite{matrixv2}, or the measurement documents \cite{rules,styles,botlab}.

\section{Addendum: the turn-pass correction}
\label{sec:addturnpass}

\emph{September 2026.} A rule marked \code{[DERIVED]} in \code{RULES\_US54.md} \S4 was wrong: a
declare that emptied the current turn-holder advanced the turn to the next seat holding cards in
ascending cyclic order, which is an opponent whenever that opponent holds a card, where the
owner's rulebook passes it to a teammate. Measured over 400 games, 19 of 56 such events gave the
turn to the wrong team --- about one game in twenty-one. The engine is corrected, the rules hash
moved, and every artifact was regenerated \cite{rules}.

\textbf{The theorem is untouched, because it is a theorem.} Section~\ref{sec:theorem}'s argument
needs one empirical fact --- that Punter's row of the measured counter table dominates every
column --- and the counter table regenerated from the re-measured matrix still gives
$\mathrm{BR}(i) = \textsc{punter}$ for all nine $i$. Expected payoff is still linear in belief, so
the warm best response is still Punter under every possible read, and the oracle ablation still
prices perfect classification at exactly $0.0000$ in all nine cells: handed the true styles, the
agent still plays move-for-move identical games.

\subsection{The isolation, and the warmup tax}

The gauntlet and the mixed screen were re-run twice on one tree with the turn-pass rule as the only
difference. \textbf{No gauntlet cell moves further than $0.0032$, every move is inside that cell's
own standard error, and the mixed screen moves $0.0011$ against its own $\pm 0.0042$.} The
correction is invisible to this instrument, exactly as it is to the matrix and the bit ladder
\cite{v05paper, matrixv2}.

What did move is the level, and the cause is the concession layer of v2.0, which
postdates the committed artifact this paper reports: every roster style now carries \code{defuse}
$=1$, and both arms of every cell delegate to the roster.

\textbf{The direction is unchanged and the evidence is weaker, and this paper says so rather than
quoting the stronger run.} All nine gauntlet cells still sit below the paired Punter benchmark,
which is the fact P1 was registered to test, and P1 returns CONFIRMED. But no single cell now
rejects at the Bonferroni bound $|z| > 2.773$, where the published run had one at $z$ $-3.53$; and
the mixed screen's interval, $[-0.0157, +0.0008]$, now \emph{includes} zero where the published
one did not.

The conclusion of this paper does not rest on either margin. It rests on the dominance theorem,
which is unaffected, and on the oracle ablation, which is exactly zero in both runs and is the
sharper result: \textbf{perfect classification buys nothing, and a warmup that buys nothing still
costs something.} What the regeneration removes is the ability to say how much, at this sample.
The warmup tax is now a direction with a bounded magnitude rather than a resolved number, and
Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}'s claim should be read at that strength.
